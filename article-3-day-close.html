How to close your books in 3 days (not 3 weeks)

A practical playbook for a faster month-end: daily reconciliation, smart accruals and the checklist we use for more than 140 clients.

PNPriya Nair
Partner, Bookkeeping & Tax

For most growing businesses, month-end close takes two to three weeks. By the time the numbers arrive, the month they describe is long gone and so is the chance to act on them.

It doesn't have to be that way. Across our clients, the average close is now three working days. Here's how, and how you can do the same.

1. Reconcile every day, not every month

The biggest reason closes are slow is that reconciliation is left until the end. Thirty days of transactions are matched in one go, and every mismatch has to be investigated from memory.

Reconciling daily turns one huge task into thirty small ones. Mismatches are caught while the context is fresh. With AI-assisted matching, most days need only a few minutes of review.

Rule of thumb: if more than 5% of your bank lines are unmatched on day 1 of the close, your close will take more than a week.

2. Decide your cut-off rules in advance

Arguments about which month an expense belongs to waste days. Write down your rules once:

  • Invoices received after day 2 of the new month are accrued, not chased.
  • Expenses under a set threshold are recognised when paid.
  • Recurring costs such as rent, software and retainers are accrued automatically.

3. Use a close calendar with owners

A close is a project. Give it a calendar with one owner per task:

DayTaskOwner
Day 1Final bank & card reconciliation, payroll entriesAccountant
Day 2Accruals, prepaid items, depreciation, intercompanyAccountant
Day 3Review, variance analysis, sign-off and MIS packFinance lead

4. Review variances, not every line

Once reconciliation is automated, review time should go where it matters: the items that changed. Compare each P&L line with last month and with the budget, and investigate anything that moved by more than an agreed threshold.

"The goal isn't to look at every number. It's to know which numbers deserve a look."

5. Keep a human in the loop

AI handles volume brilliantly, but it can't tell you whether a one-off payment was a good decision. Every month at Dair ends with a qualified accountant reviewing the exceptions and signing off the close, and with a short call to explain what the numbers mean.

The 3-day close checklist

  1. Connect every bank, card and payment gateway to a daily feed.
  2. Automate categorisation and matching, and review exceptions daily.
  3. Document your cut-off and accrual rules.
  4. Run a close calendar with named owners.
  5. Review variances, then sign off.
