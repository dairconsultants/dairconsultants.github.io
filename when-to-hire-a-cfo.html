When does a growing business need a CFO?

Signs that it is time for senior finance support, and how to get it without a full-time hire.

Article · 6 min read·By the DAIR Consultants team

In the early days, the founder usually handles the money, with a bookkeeper and an accountant for compliance. As the business grows, the questions get harder, and that is often the moment to bring in senior finance help.

Signs it may be time

  • You are often unsure how much cash you will have in three months.
  • You are planning to raise funds or take a large loan, and need a model that will stand up to questions.
  • Revenue is growing but profit is not, and you cannot see why.
  • Big decisions are waiting on numbers, such as hiring, pricing, a new location or a new product.
  • Your monthly reports arrive late, or nobody explains what they mean.

What senior finance support does

A good finance lead turns your accounts into decisions. That usually means a budget, a rolling forecast, a clear view of cash, and a short monthly report that highlights what has changed and why.

Full-time or part-time?

Many growing businesses do not need a full-time finance head yet. A part-time or consulting arrangement gives you senior experience for the few days a month you actually need it, and can grow into a full-time role later.

How to start

  1. Make sure your books are accurate and up to date. Advice is only as good as the numbers behind it.
  2. Write down the three decisions you most need help with this year.
  3. Agree a clear scope, a regular rhythm of meetings and a fixed fee.

This guide is general information, not advice for your specific situation. Rules and due dates can change, so please check the latest position or talk to us before acting.
