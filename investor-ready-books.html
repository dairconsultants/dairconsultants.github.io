Getting your books ready for an investor

What investors usually look for in your financial records, and how to prepare before the conversation starts.

Guide · 6 min read·By the DAIR Consultants team

When an investor becomes interested, they will ask to see your financial records. Clean, consistent books make that process faster and build confidence. Messy books slow it down and raise questions.

What investors usually ask for

  • Monthly profit and loss statements and balance sheets for the recent past.
  • Bank statements and reconciliations that match your books.
  • A list of customers and suppliers, with outstanding amounts.
  • Tax filings and proof that statutory payments are up to date.
  • A forecast showing how you plan to use the money and grow.

Five steps to prepare

  1. Close every month properly. Reconcile bank accounts, record accruals and review the results.
  2. Separate business and personal spending. Mixed transactions are one of the most common problems investors find.
  3. Check your compliance. Make sure GST, TDS and company filings are complete and on time.
  4. Organise your documents. Keep contracts, invoices and filings in a tidy, shared folder.
  5. Build a simple forecast. Show your assumptions clearly and keep them realistic.

Start early

Getting books into shape takes time, especially if past months need catching up. Starting a few months before you speak to investors makes the whole process calmer.

This guide is general information, not advice for your specific situation. Rules and due dates can change, so please check the latest position or talk to us before acting.
